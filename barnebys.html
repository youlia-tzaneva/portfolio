Modernizing a marketplace at scale

Introduction

Barnebys is a global auction marketplace connecting users with auction houses, collectibles, art, and rare objects across multiple international markets. Operating across 8 domains in the US, Europe, and Asia, the platform supports a diverse ecosystem of auction-related products under the wider Barnebys Group.

 

As the sole designer leading the project, I was responsible for modernizing the experience while preserving the credibility and established feel expected within the auction industry. My role extended beyond interface design, spanning research, systems thinking, stakeholder alignment, design strategy, and implementation support.

The challenge

Barnebys faced a unique balancing act.

Inconsistent user experience

Without a design system, interfaces varied significantly across pages, devices, and the Barnebys Group platforms.

Legacy design constraints

The platform needed modernization without losing credibility in a trust-driven marketplace.

Competing priorities

Different teams had conflicting perspectives on design direction, functionality, and business priorities.

The platform needed to feel: modern yet established, minimal yet content-rich, scalable yet consistent across markets. At the same time, the experience had become fragmented.

Defining success

To guide the redesign, I aligned decisions around three principles:

Build trust through clarity

Create a more professional and cohesive experience to cater to a diverse audience.

Design for scalability

Establish reusable systems that could support multiple products and international domains.

Improve discoverability and retention

Make browsing, searching, and search alerts more intuitive and engaging.

Discovery

Before redesigning the experience, I focused on understanding both user and business pain points. Research included a competitor analysis, heuristic evaluations, user interviews, and platform audits. I synthesized findings into actionable insights and presented user interview outcomes at a company-wide level to build broader alignment around customer needs.

A key insight emerged from my discussions with users:

Users valued the breadth of Barnebys' offering, but struggled with clarity, navigation, and consistency across experiences.

This surfaced a larger systems issue:

The platform felt like multiple disconnected experiences rather than one cohesive product ecosystem.

Building a scalable design foundation

One of the biggest opportunities was addressing the lack of consistency across the platform. I created a design system from scratch, defining a scalable color system with semantic states and use cases, typography hierarchy and spacing rules, reusable components, an icon system, and responsive behavior across devices.

 

The goal was not only visual consistency, but faster decision-making and a stronger foundation for future product growth.

Driving stakeholder alignment

As priorities differed across departments, design feedback often became fragmented and contradictory. To move the project forward, I organized and facilitated a design workshop with department leads to align on redesign goals, product priorities, visual direction, and brand tone. This created stronger alignment and helped establish a clearer shared vision for the redesign moving forward.

AI-assisted workflows

After completing the core designs, I began contributing to a new product offering using an AI-assisted workflow. Using Cursor, I translated Figma designs into functional front-end code, working closely with developers to improve implementation speed, reduce ambiguity, and increase fidelity between designs and shipped experiences. This closer collaboration strengthened alignment between design and engineering while improving the efficiency of the handoff process.

Impact

The redesign established a stronger, cleaner, and more scalable product foundation across Barnebys' ecosystem.

Success metrics are coming soon following our recent site launch!

Reflection

This project taught me that successful product design depends as much on alignment as execution. Looking back, one of the biggest challenges was navigating conflicting priorities without a clearly defined decision framework. Too much time was spent reacting to individual preferences rather than aligning around shared user and business goals.

 

Going forward, I would establish stronger alignment earlier through more collaborative planning, clearer success criteria, and structured feedback processes tied to expertise and measurable outcomes. Most importantly, this experience reinforced the value of creating clarity, both in products and in the teams building them.
